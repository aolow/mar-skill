# Scientific writing convention

Write for the scientist revising the manuscript. Use concise, direct sentences
and familiar words. Retain necessary scientific terms and define abbreviations at
first use. Avoid unnecessary jargon, stock praise, dramatic verdicts, rhetorical
questions and speculation about author motives.

For each substantive assessment:

1. State what the manuscript reports, with a source location or evidence ID beside
   the statement. Preserve exact quotations, numbers and units.
2. Explain what that result supports. Include the study setting, population or
   data, comparator and independent unit when they affect interpretation. Retain
   reported effect sizes and uncertainty when needed; do not invent missing context.
3. State the limit. Distinguish association from causation, prediction from
   treatment benefit, and reported procedures from verified execution. Label
   inference and unverified external claims. Cite relevant counterevidence.
4. Give the next check or revision and say what its result would change.

Keep summary statements linked to the detailed assessment. Do not turn a
verification question into a confirmed defect, remove context to shorten prose,
or treat a source citation as proof of an interpretation. Use neutral headings
such as “Main concerns,” “Open verification questions” and “Next step.”

For example, replace “The scaling story is unconvincing” with “Training size and
tissue composition vary together, so this comparison does not isolate the effect
of training size,” followed by the relevant source reference. Replace “A promising
clinical biomarker” with the specific reported association and its limitation:
“The marker is associated with nonresponse to the treatments studied; benefit
from the proposed treatment remains untested,” citing both statements.

Apply this convention to the summary, detailed themes and conversational report.
It guides newly authored prose; never paraphrase an evidence quote in place or
change an engine finding's status to make the wording simpler.
