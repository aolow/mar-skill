# Evidence challenge before final triage

Read this when turning a CLI report or labeled model-native review into the final
author checklist. Use one bounded pass over the strongest candidate concerns and
strengths. Test the reviewer's reasoning against the available manuscript.

For each proposed headline theme:

1. **Claim and stakes:** identify the actual manuscript claim and what conclusion
   would change if the concern holds. Keep a reporting gap separate from a defect.
2. **Evidence chain:** retain the engine IDs and exact source passage(s). Distinguish
   the quoted observation, reviewer inference, and any external premise. An exact
   quote verifies text presence, not the inference made from it.
3. **Challenge:** inspect available source context for the strongest rebuttal or
   counterevidence, including supplements. Do not invent either. If not found, say
   “not located in the supplied material”; this does not prove it does not exist.
   Check whether apparently independent results reuse donors, labels, construction
   features, reference atlases, or underlying measurements.
4. **Decision:** retain, narrow, or recommend human verification of the theme. State
   what evidence would change that recommendation. Preserve a credible minority
   concern and unresolved disagreement; do not resolve disputes by counting roles.
5. **Next check:** give the least costly discriminating analysis or clarification,
   its required data, biological unit, comparison, metric and decision criterion
   where relevant. If inputs are unavailable or unknown, label the dependency or
   proposed new experiment instead of calling it an executable reanalysis.

Apply the same scrutiny to major strengths: what was reported, what it supports,
and what it cannot establish. Limit headline themes to those that survive this
pass. Leave weak or externally dependent leads visible in a short verification
list. Preserve the engine records; a triage recommendation is not a mutation of
their disposition or confidence.

## Research rationale and validation boundary

The [Virtual Biotech](https://virtualbiotech.ai/) uses specialist analysis followed
by scientific review and synthesis. Its
[February 2026 preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12970349/)
describes a reviewer that checks relevance, evidence strength and thoroughness,
and notes that agent errors can propagate despite that review. The final paper
was [published in Science in September 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg6779).

The [Virtual Lab study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09442-9) combines specialist
roles, critique, human guidance and experimental testing in a nanobody-design task.
Neither paper establishes that this checklist improves manuscript peer review.
This pass is an adaptation to test, not a validated scientific-quality upgrade.
It adds no reviewer personas or automatic provider calls.

Evaluate the revised checklist against the prior workflow on the same held-out
manuscripts and fixed provider outputs, with blinded expert ratings for important
correct concerns, unsupported concerns, missed issues, action feasibility, and
author usefulness. Include cases where a single well-supported concern conflicts
with several weak ones and where the manuscript rebuts an apparent concern.
